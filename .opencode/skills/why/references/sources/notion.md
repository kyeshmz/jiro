# Notion Docs

## What this source contains

- PRDs, technical specs, and RFCs.
- Architectural decision records.
- Design-review meeting notes.
- Team pages and domain context.
- Incident postmortems and runbooks.
- Strategy documents explaining priorities.

Documents may capture rationale written before implementation.

## How to search it

Discover the active document MCP and exact schemas. Names such as `notion-search`, `notion-fetch`, `notion-query-data-sources`, and `notion-query-meeting-notes` are examples. Adapt to Confluence, Google Docs, or Coda when available.

1. **Search keywords.** Use feature names, symbols, author handles, error strings, and user-visible terms. Bound by dates when supported.
2. **Read candidate pages fully.** Do not rely on previews.
3. **Follow document links.** Alternatives, appendices, and implementation notes may live in child pages.
4. **Inspect related databases and meeting notes.** These may record the actual decision.
5. **Search author-owned spaces when accessible and within scope.** Exploratory thinking may precede the implementation.

## What good evidence looks like here

- A matching problem statement or motivation section.
- Alternatives considered or rejected approaches.
- A postmortem naming the target as a corrective action.
- Meeting notes recording a choice and reason.
- A completed ADR with context, decision, and consequences.

## Common pitfalls

- **Outdated docs.** The implementation may differ from the original plan.
- **Doc/reality drift.** Surface divergences rather than silently choosing a source.
- **Boilerplate.** A generic why section is weak evidence.
- **Unlinked docs.** Broad keyword searches may be necessary.
- **Several drafts.** Inspect finalization and update dates.
- **Access restrictions.** Report unavailable pages as gaps.

## What to return

Give title, retrieved URL, authors, dates, verbatim motivation with section location, linked-page leads, and draft/final status.
